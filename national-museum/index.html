National Museum of Nepal

National Museum — Chhauni Museum

The National Museum at Chhauni, on the edge of Kathmandu, is the oldest and largest museum in the country. Founded in 1928 as an arsenal displaying weapons and war trophies, it has grown into a leading institution preserving Nepal’s art, history and natural heritage.

Set within the old military quarter of Chhauni, on high ground looking over the Kathmandu Valley, the National Museum did not begin as a place to admire art — it began as an arsenal. That legacy can still be seen in some of its oldest galleries, even though the institution has since expanded to cover centuries of Nepali sculpture, painting and natural history.

From arsenal to national museum

The museum traces its origins to 1928, when it opened inside a former military barracks complex known as Chhauni Silkhana, displaying weapons, armour and war trophies gathered from Nepal’s military campaigns, including the Anglo-Nepalese War. This first collection mirrored the military character of the site, which served administrative and military functions for a long period under Rana rule.

In later decades the museum’s scope grew far beyond weapons. Additional buildings were brought in to house sculpture, paintings, coins and archaeological objects, transforming what began as a specialised arms display into a general historical and cultural museum representing Nepal’s artistic and political history.

Today’s compound has several buildings, each with its own focus, which lets the museum present a wide range of material — from centuries-old stone carvings to taxidermied wildlife specimens — without crowding it into a single gallery space.

Collections and highlights

Among its most notable holdings, the museum has an extensive weapons collection documenting Nepal’s military history, along with stone and metal sculptures depicting Hindu and Buddhist deities that show how Nepali religious art developed over the centuries. The coin collection offers a glimpse of the economic history of the Kathmandu Valley’s historic kingdoms before the unified Nepali rupee.

A separate Buddhist art gallery in the compound focuses on thangka paintings, bronze statues and religious objects, while the neighbouring Natural History Museum, though operated separately, shares the same historic compound and displays specimens of Nepal’s flora and fauna.

✦ Did you know?

  • The museum compound stands on the grounds of a former military barracks, and some of its buildings still bear traces of that original use.
  • The name Chhauni comes from a word associated with military barracks of the Rana administration.
  • The weapons gallery includes guns and swords used in the early-19th-century Anglo-Nepalese War.

Frequently asked questions

What is the National Museum of Nepal?

The National Museum of Nepal at Chhauni, Kathmandu is the country’s oldest and largest museum, holding weapons, art, coins and historical objects spanning Nepal’s history.

When was the National Museum of Nepal established?

The museum began in 1928 (1985 BS), when it opened as an arms museum displaying weapons and war trophies; it later grew into a general historical and cultural museum.

Where is the National Museum located?

The National Museum of Nepal is at Chhauni on the western edge of Kathmandu, inside a historic complex once used for military purposes.

What can visitors see at the National Museum?

Visitors can see historic weapons and armour, sculptures and artworks reflecting Nepal’s Buddhist and Hindu traditions, coins, and separate buildings devoted to natural history and Buddhist art.

Is the National Museum different from other museums in Kathmandu?

Yes. The National Museum at Chhauni is distinct from other Kathmandu Valley museums such as the Patan Museum and the Hanuman Dhoka Palace Museum, and is regarded as the principal historical museum representing Nepal at the national level.
