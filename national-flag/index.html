National Symbol · ध्वजा

The Only National Flag on Earth That Isn't a Rectangle

Nepal's flag is built from two stacked crimson pennants, crowned with a moon and a sun. It is the single non-quadrilateral national flag in use anywhere in the world — a geometric fact, not a metaphor, fixed by formula in Nepal's constitution.

In short

The national flag of Nepal is the world's only non-rectangular national flag, formed of two stacked crimson pennants bearing a moon and a sun, bordered in blue. Its geometric construction is fixed precisely by Nepal's constitution. The current simplified form was codified in 1962; the crimson echoes the rhododendron, Nepal's national flower, and the blue border represents peace.

About Nepal's National Flag

A Flag Defined by a Formula, Not a Free Hand

Most national flags are rectangles filled with stripes, crosses or charges. Nepal's is the lone exception: two pennants, one stacked above the other, inspired by Hindu dhvaja religious banners and, in many accounts, created in the time of Prithvi Narayan Shah. Where most flags can be redrawn loosely and still pass, Nepal's constitution fixes its outline with an explicit geometric construction — meaning that, in principle, no two correctly drawn Nepali flags can disagree on shape.

That unusual rigor is part of what makes the flag a genuine outlier in vexillology, the study of flags: it is simultaneously the most mathematically defined and the most visually distinct flag in use by any country today.

Construction

Geometry of the Flag

  1. Draw the left edge (the flagpole side) as a straight vertical line of a chosen length.
  2. From its base, mark a point partway up to begin the lower pennant's diagonal.
  3. Construct the upper pennant as a triangle, then the lower pennant as a second, slightly larger triangle directly beneath it.
  4. Trim the outer points of both pennants using a defined offset, producing the flag's signature folded-looking tips.
  5. Inset a blue border of fixed width along the entire outline.
  6. Place the moon emblem in the centre of the upper pennant and the sun emblem in the centre of the lower pennant.

This step-by-step geometric method (the steps above are a simplified summary — Schedule 1 gives the official construction) is unusual among national flags — most countries specify proportions, not full geometric construction. The approach guarantees the same silhouette regardless of who draws it, which is part of why the shape has stayed unchanged even as the emblems inside it were simplified in 1962.

Emblems

Why the Sun and Moon?

Moon

Set in the upper pennant, the moon stands for Nepal's cool, shaded climate and carries the hope that the nation will endure for as long as the moon exists in the sky.

Sun

Set in the lower pennant, the sun stands for the intense heat of the southern lowlands and the same wish for permanence, mirroring the moon's symbolism.

Origins

History of the Flag

The double pennant is a combination of two individual pennons used by rival branches of the ruling dynasty. Many accounts credit King Prithvi Narayan Shah with creating it after unification, and its shape is inspired by Hindu dhvaja religious banners.

Earlier versions of the flag carried human faces in the sun and moon (some historians credit Jung Bahadur Rana with introducing them). The faces were removed in 1962, when the current design was adopted with the new constitution.

Through History

Timeline

Pre-Unification Era

Triangular red pennons are used as banners by the rulers of the hill principalities, including Gorkha.

Unification Period

After Prithvi Narayan Shah unifies the principalities, the double pennant becomes the standard flag.

19th–20th Century

The sun and moon carry human faces; some historians credit Jung Bahadur Rana with introducing them.

1962

The faces are removed to modernise the flag, and the 1962 constitution adopts the current design on 16 December.

Present Day

The 1990 and 2015 constitutions both contain geometric specifications for the flag, which is still cited internationally as the only non-rectangular national flag.

Protection & Protocol

Legal Status & Flag Protocol

  • 1
    Constitutional definition: Schedule 1 of the Constitution of Nepal sets out a specific method of making the national flag.
  • 2
    Correct construction: official versions are expected to follow the prescribed geometric method rather than free reproduction.
  • 3
    Respectful display (general flag etiquette): the flag is expected to be flown intact and undamaged, and never allowed to touch the ground.
  • 4
    National observances (general practice): the flag is prominently displayed on government buildings during national holidays and official ceremonies.
