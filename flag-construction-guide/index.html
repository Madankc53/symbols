Flag Construction Guide

How Nepal's Flag Is Built

Schedule 1 of the Constitution builds the flag in 24 steps and four parts: the shape, the moon, the sun and the border. No other national flag is written down this way.

Nepal's flag is not drawn from a simple width-to-height ratio. Schedule 1 of the Constitution of Nepal gives a method of 24 numbered steps, built from straight lines, arcs and circles. This page lets you watch it being built in 3D and explains how the method is organised. It does not replace the legal text.

Read all 24 steps as text
  1. Base line. On crimson cloth, draw a line AB from left to right. Its length sets the scale, so AB = 1.
  2. Height and point D. Raise AC at right angles to AB, with AC = AB plus one third of AB. Mark D on AC so that AD = AB, then join B and D.
  3. Point E. On BD, mark E so that BE = AB.
  4. Line FG. Through E, draw FG parallel to AB, starting at F on AC, with FG = AB.
  5. Join C and G. The outline is now complete: two stacked pennants.
  6. Points H and I. Mark H on AB with AH = one quarter of AB. Draw HI parallel to AC, meeting CG at I.
  7. Bisect CF. Find the midpoint J of CF and draw JK parallel to AB, meeting CG at K.
  8. Point L. L is where JK and HI cross.
  9. Join J and G. Draw the line JG.
  10. Point M. M is where JG meets HI.
  11. Point N. With centre M and the shortest distance from M to BD as radius, mark N on HI below M.
  12. Line OM. Through M, draw a line parallel to AB, starting at O on AC.
  13. Arc PNQ. With centre L and radius LN, draw the lower semicircle. P and Q are where it meets line OM.
  14. Outer edge of the crescent. With centre M and radius MQ, draw the lower semicircle through P and Q.
  15. Points R, S and T. With centre N and radius NM, draw an arc meeting arc PNQ at R and S. Join RS; T is where RS meets HI.
  16. Outer boundary of the rays. With centre T and radius TS, draw the upper semicircle.
  17. Inner boundary of the rays. With centre T and radius TM, draw the upper arc.
  18. Eight moon rays. Make eight equal, similar triangles in the space between the two arcs.
  19. Points U and V. Bisect AF at U and draw UV parallel to AB, meeting BE at V.
  20. Inner circle of the sun. W is where UV meets HI. With centre W and radius MN, draw a circle.
  21. Outer circle of the sun. With centre W and radius LN, draw a second circle.
  22. Twelve sun rays. Make twelve equal, similar triangles in the ring between the circles, with two apexes touching HI.
  23. The border. The deep blue border is as wide as TN and runs along all sides. At the five angles of the flag, the outer angles equal the inner ones.
  24. Rope or pole. The border is made as described when the flag is hung from a rope. For a pole, the hole in the border alongside AC can be extended as needed.

Geometry follows Schedule 1 (Article 8) of the Constitution of Nepal 2015: all points A to W are calculated from the clauses with AB = 1, and the animation’s proportions are checked against the flag’s published height-to-width ratio of about 1.219. Schedule 1 leaves the real-world size to the Government of Nepal, and on-screen size here is arbitrary. The Constitution says “crimson” and “deep blue” only; the on-screen colours are common digital approximations, not official values. Point letters follow the English text. Read the original on Constitute Project or Nepal Laws.

The 24 Steps in Four Parts

1. The shape1–5A base line AB on crimson cloth, a perpendicular AC, and the points that give the two-pennant outline
2. The moon6–18Semicircles and arcs that fix the crescent, with eight equal triangles inside it
3. The sun19–22Two circles, with twelve equal triangles in the ring between them
4. The border23–24A deep blue border on all sides, whose width equals the segment TN

Source: Constitution of Nepal 2015, Schedule 1 (relating to Article 8), as reproduced on Constitute Project and the Nepal Law Commission's published text.

Part 1: The Shape

The method starts on the lower part of a crimson cloth with a base line AB drawn from left to right. A perpendicular AC is then raised from A. Its length is the length of AB plus one third of AB, so the flag is taller than it is wide. Further points on these lines are joined to give the outline of two stacked triangles, the shape often called two pennants.

Nepal's flag is the only national flag in the world that is not a rectangle or square. Schedule 1 is what keeps its outline identical wherever it is made.

Parts 2 and 3: The Moon and the Sun

The crescent moon takes the longest part of the method, 13 of the 24 steps. Points are marked from measured distances and crossing lines, and semicircles and arcs are drawn from them. The crescent lies between one semicircle and one arc, and eight equal and similar triangles are made in it.

The sun is drawn lower down. A line is bisected, two circles are drawn from the same centre, and twelve equal triangles fill the ring between them. Each pair of apexes touches a construction line. The moon and sun are white and the field is crimson.

Part 4: The Border

The border is deep blue and runs along every side of the flag. Its width equals the length TN from the construction. The outer corners repeat the angles of the inner ones. For a flag hung from a pole rather than a rope, Schedule 1 allows the border along the hoist side to be extended to make a sleeve.

Lines You Never See

Many of the lines and arcs in the method are imaginary guides. They exist only to place points and do not appear on the finished flag, apart from the edge of the crescent moon. The size of the flag is not fixed in Schedule 1. It is left to the Government of Nepal to decide.

Why It Is Written in the Constitution

The flag was adopted on 16 December 1962 with the constitution of that year. Writing the full construction into the constitution means any maker can reproduce the same flag from the text alone. Today the flag is named in Article 8 and its drawing method is in Schedule 1 of the 2015 Constitution. The colours and meaning are covered on the National Flag and National Colour pages.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many steps are in the construction of Nepal's flag?

Schedule 1 of the Constitution of Nepal gives 24 numbered steps, grouped as the shape (steps 1–5), the moon (6–18), the sun (19–22) and the border (23–24).

Why is Nepal's flag not a rectangle?

Its outline is two stacked triangular pennants. It is the only national flag in the world that is neither a rectangle nor a square, and Schedule 1 fixes the exact shape.

How many rays do the sun and moon have?

The moon has eight equal triangles and the sun has twelve, as set by Schedule 1.

What colour and width is the border?

Deep blue, running on all sides. Its width equals the segment called TN in the construction.

Where can I read the official text?

In Schedule 1 of the Constitution of Nepal 2015, available on Constitute Project and from the Nepal Law Commission. This page animates and explains it, but the legal text is the authority.

Is the 3D animation accurate?

Every point from A to W is calculated from the clauses of Schedule 1, and the resulting height-to-width ratio comes out at about 1.219, the flag's published ratio. The on-screen colours are digital approximations.
