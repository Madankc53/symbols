Where Snow Leopards Live in Nepal

Snow leopards live in the high Himalaya, mostly at 3,000 to 5,500 metres. A telemetry study by WWF in the Kangchenjunga Conservation Area found that most collared animals preferred about 13,000 to 16,000 feet (roughly 4,000 to 4,900 metres), and the highest point recorded was 19,186 feet. Three of four collared animals crossed into China or India for part of their time, so protection depends on neighbouring countries too. Our national parks page lists the Himalayan parks, including Shey Phoksundo, Sagarmatha and Langtang.

Much of the habitat is unprotected. The director general of the Department of Forests and Soil Conservation said a significant share of snow leopard habitat lies outside protected areas.

Source: Down To Earth; WWF, "This year brings hopeful news for Nepal's snow leopards". The elevation range for the species is general knowledge and not from these reports.

Threats

WWF names habitat loss and fragmentation from infrastructure, climate change and human-wildlife conflict as the main pressures, and poaching is described as a persistent threat to the species. The IUCN lists the snow leopard as Vulnerable.

✦ Did you know?

  • The estimate is described as the first robust national figure, so earlier numbers you may see online were rough guesses.
  • Snow leopards share Nepal's mountains with Mount Everest. See our Mount Everest page.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many snow leopards are there in Nepal?

An estimated 397, according to the first national estimate released in April 2025.

Who counted the snow leopards in Nepal?

A report by the Department of National Parks and Wildlife Conservation and the Department of Forests and Soil Conservation, announced with WWF.

How was the snow leopard population estimated?

With camera traps and genetic analysis of scat, using data collected across seven study regions from 2015 to 2024.

How many snow leopards are there in India?

India's January 2024 status report estimated 718 snow leopards.

Are snow leopards endangered?

The IUCN lists the snow leopard as Vulnerable. Main threats include habitat loss, climate change and human-wildlife conflict.

Where do snow leopards live in Nepal?

In the high Himalaya, including protected areas such as Kangchenjunga Conservation Area. A significant part of their habitat is outside protected areas.
