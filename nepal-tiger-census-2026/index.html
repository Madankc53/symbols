Nepal has 429 wild tigers, according to the 2026 national tiger census. That is a record, up 74 from 355 in 2022. The Department of National Parks and Wildlife Conservation released the result on Global Tiger Day, 29 July 2026.

Chitwan remains the largest tiger habitat. The biggest jumps were in Parsa and Banke. Bardiya was the only park to decline, from 125 to 112. Read more about the parks on our national parks of Nepal page, and compare with the rhino count and snow leopard estimate in our wildlife counts guide.

Source: The Kathmandu Post, "Habitat concerns grow as Nepal counts a record 429 tigers" (30 July 2026); IUCN (29 July 2026).

How the Tigers Were Counted

The census used camera traps and ran from 31 December 2025 to 23 April 2026, according to the Kathmandu Post. The IUCN says the Department of National Parks and Wildlife Conservation ran it with the Department of Forests and Soil Conservation, provincial governments, the Nepal Army and conservation partners. It is run every four years, so the next full census would be expected around 2030.

The Other Side: Conflict and Space

More tigers also means more conflict. The NTNC reported 10 people killed by tigers in fiscal year 2025-26, the first rise after four years of decline: Banke 4, Parsa 3, Bardiya 2 and Chitwan 1. Over eight years, 85 people were killed. Wildlife expert Hem Sagar Baral told the Post that Nepal could support roughly 400 to 500 tigers, so the country may be nearing its limit.

Source: The Kathmandu Post, 30 July 2026.

✦ Did you know?

  • The tiger is not Nepal's national animal. The Constitution names the cow. See our national animal page.
  • Nepal's 2026 count is more than three times the 121 tigers counted in 2009.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many tigers are there in Nepal in 2026?

The 2026 national tiger census counted 429 tigers in Nepal, a record and up from 355 in 2022.

Which national park in Nepal has the most tigers?

Chitwan National Park and its buffer zone, with 145 tigers in the 2026 census, followed by Bardiya (112) and Parsa (71).

How many tigers did Nepal have in 2022?

Nepal had 355 tigers in the 2022 census.

Are tigers increasing in Nepal?

Yes. The count rose from 121 in 2009 to 429 in 2026. Four of the five tiger parks increased in 2026; Bardiya fell from 125 to 112.

When was the Nepal tiger census 2026 released?

It was released on Global Tiger Day, 29 July 2026, in Kathmandu.

How was the 2026 tiger census done?

With camera traps, in a survey that ran from 31 December 2025 to 23 April 2026.

When is the next tiger census in Nepal?

The census is run every four years, so the next one is expected around 2030.

Is the tiger the national animal of Nepal?

No. The national animal under Nepal's Constitution is the cow.
