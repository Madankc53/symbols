How the Rhino Population Has Changed

The 2015 census counted 645 rhinos. The March 2021 census counted 752, an increase of 107, and included 136 calves. Save the Rhino reports that 161 rhinos died between 2015 and 2021, six of them killed by poachers. Mongabay reports that growth slowed from about 5 to 6 percent a year in 2011-2018 to about 3 percent a year in 2018-2021.

Source: Save the Rhino; Mongabay (May 2025).

Why There Is No New Rhino Census Yet

Nepal counts rhinos about every five years, and the last count was in 2021. A census scheduled for 2081 BS was delayed because of budget problems, equipment shortages and technical discussions, according to eKantipur. Mongabay reported in 2025 that a funding shortfall after USAID support was withdrawn halted it, at an estimated cost of 115,000 to 155,000 US dollars.

The department is now looking at a new method. The current one uses trained elephants to carry teams through the grassland, which is dangerous: in 2021 a technician was injured when a wild elephant charged a survey elephant. Options under study include dung DNA analysis, camera traps and a mix of both. A committee will first write a protocol and run pilot studies, and no date has been announced.

The figure of 752 is five years old. Until a new census is published, 752 remains the official number, even though the true population today may be higher or lower.

Source: eKantipur (17 April 2026); Mongabay (May 2025); The Kathmandu Post (23 September 2026).

✦ Did you know?

  • The greater one-horned rhino is listed as Vulnerable by the IUCN and lives in tall grassland and riverine forest, such as along the Rapti river in Chitwan.
  • Nepal counted a record 429 tigers in 2026. See the tiger census page and the 13 national parks.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many rhinos are there in Nepal?

Nepal had 752 greater one-horned rhinoceroses in the March 2021 census, the latest official count.

Which national park in Nepal has the most rhinos?

Chitwan National Park, with 694 of the 752 rhinos in the 2021 census.

How many rhinos were there in Nepal in 2015?

The 2015 census counted 645 rhinos.

When is the next rhino census in Nepal?

No date has been announced. It was due after 2021 but has been delayed, and the department is redesigning the method.

Why was Nepal's rhino census delayed?

Because of budget problems, equipment shortages and technical discussions about the method, according to eKantipur. Mongabay linked a 2025 halt to lost USAID funding.

How are rhinos counted in Nepal?

By direct sightings from the back of trained elephants. Dung DNA and camera traps are being considered for the next census.

Are rhinos endangered in Nepal?

The greater one-horned rhino is classed as Vulnerable by the IUCN. Nepal's population grew from 645 in 2015 to 752 in 2021.

In which national parks do rhinos live in Nepal?

Chitwan, Bardiya, Shuklaphanta and Parsa, according to the 2021 census.
